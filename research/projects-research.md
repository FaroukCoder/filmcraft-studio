## The blunt version

- **Creative Studio is the right top priority, and the LLM is not where its quality comes from.** In a September 2026 benchmark of agents editing raw footage, the best resolved 27 % of tasks, and most failures were on quality alone (headline figures; I couldn't open the full paper). Your edge is signal (beats, drops, phrases, pose, Serato's own logs) plus your taste. Use the model to fill in a plan, not to judge one. The plumbing works on Linux with synthetic footage (Windows untested): I built your fork and drove it headless from a JSON plan to a finished 9:16 clip with cuts, a reframe and burned-in Arabic captions (Top 10 #5).
- **You've already moved the router off Laya onto EmbeddingGemma, and that was the right call.** Shipped Laya scores below "always pick the commonest answer" on its own benchmark (0.36 vs 0.46). Day 1 is now one evening proving the new router on 200 of your routes.
- **A 12 GB local agent will not be as reliable as Claude Code.** Expect a few percent of tool turns to need a retry. Use Hermes for routing, automation and vault work; keep heavy coding on Claude Code.
- **Don't build the Inspiration Canvas app.** Store boards as JSON Canvas in your vault (or buy Eagle for $35) and spend the time on Creative Studio.
- **Don't take whatstheID public.** The "paste a mix, get a tracklist" space is crowded and the download features carry legal risk. Finish the Serato cue write-back (Top 10 #10) for your own prep, not as an edge: Mixed In Key has auto-set Serato cues since 2015.
- **Generated footage (MiniMax H3) doesn't belong in paid club clips:** the product is that it was real. **Qwen-Image 2.1 doesn't belong in anything you sell** (non-commercial licence, Top 10 #1); flyers and announcement stills from FLUX.2 klein 4B are fine.
- **The GPU rule can be enforced, not left to habit, from Day 3.** llama-swap plus a small guard makes every model behind it answer "not now" while Serato or Ableton runs. Tested against the real llama-swap on Linux, not yet Windows (Top 10 #2); that test caught a version that would have *loaded* ComfyUI mid-set. Ollama stays outside it until you quit it.
- **Five projects plus a FilmCraft fork is too many for one person.** Week 1 is Creative Studio, Hermes (router check) and the shared GPU setup, plus Reel Brain's looping JSON (it shares the GPU and the vision model). whatstheID's code waits for week 2 (cue write-back); the canvas waits.

### 2. Put every GPU server behind llama-swap, plus a small DJ-mode guard
- **Why:** every project competes for one 12 GB GPU and the PC has crashed on long runs. You do not need to write a broker: [llama-swap](https://github.com/mostlygeek/llama-swap) (MIT, active, WinGet package for Windows) already starts and stops GPU servers on demand and keeps only one group loaded. It handles llama-server (LLM and VLM), whisper.cpp, stable-diffusion.cpp, audio.cpp and **ComfyUI** (a dedicated `/comfyui/` endpoint and a reserved `comfyui_auto` model ID so the browser websocket doesn't pin the GPU), plus `/upstream/<model>/` passthrough for anything else HTTP. `groups` with `exclusive: true` give the "only one big thing at a time" rule; `POST /api/models/unload` empties the card; `ttl` frees idle models.
- **Do:** (1) define each GPU workload as a llama-swap model (agent LLM, VLM, ASR, ComfyUI, and Ollama while you keep it) in one exclusive group; (2) point Hermes (`providers.llamacpp.base_url`), Reel Brain, Creative Studio and ComfyUI clients at llama-swap instead of their own servers; (3) a **DJ guard** that, while `Serato DJ Pro.exe` / `Serato DJ Lite.exe` / `Ableton Live*.exe` runs (psutil), switches llama-swap to a `dj` profile where every model answers 404 instead of loading (`PUT /api/profiles/active`), unloads everything (`POST /api/models/unload`) and re-unloads anything that starts anyway (the `/comfyui/` route ignores profiles); on exit it clears the profile, with no restarts; (4) make every batch job checkpoint per item and retry on connection refused, so the guard costs a pause, not a lost run: [`prototypes/gpu_queue.py`](prototypes/gpu_queue.py) is that queue (SQLite, one row per item; it waits out DJ mode, retries with backoff and parks repeat failures; its test kills a worker mid-item and the restart redoes only that item). Hermes cron's no-agent mode can drain the queue at night.
- **Trade-off:** Hermes' managed runtime sizes context and expert offload for you; behind llama-swap you set `--n-cpu-moe`, `-c` and the chat template yourself in the YAML. That is ten lines, once.
- **Why the DJ guard matters beyond VRAM:** NVIDIA driver DPC-latency spikes of 3–10 ms are a known cause of audio dropouts on Windows; heavy CUDA work makes it worse. Run LatencyMon once with a GPU job active and once without, while Serato plays.
- **Hardware side:** on RTX 30-series, crashes under load are often millisecond transients that a power cap does not stop. Cheap things to try: cap the core clock (`nvidia-smi -lgc 210,1700` as admin locks it between 210 and 1,700 MHz; pick the upper number a little under the peak `clocks.gr` your own log shows, or use an Afterburner curve), and watch **GDDR6X memory junction temperature** in HWiNFO during a long LLM or Whisper run (memory-bound work behaves like mining, and forum reports put 3080-class junctions past 100 °C there; [one pad mod](https://www.igorslab.de/en/simple-pad-mod-for-the-force-rtx-3080-founders-edition-slowers-the-gddr6x-temperature-by-a-whopping-8-degrees/) took 8 °C off). If junction temps are high, thermal pads; if crashes persist at a capped clock, suspect the PSU.
- **Tested:** I built llama-swap from source and ran the committed config with stand-in servers: asking for `vlm` unloads `agent` (one GPU owner), `/comfyui/` loads ComfyUI, transcription requests reach the QwenCleo entry, and the guard's DJ mode returns 404 for every model, empties the card, unloads a ComfyUI job that sneaks in, and restores service afterwards ([`test_dj_guard_llama_swap.py`](prototypes/test_dj_guard_llama_swap.py)). (Its first version would have loaded ComfyUI during your set; 5, item 4.)
- **Confidence: medium.** The mechanics are tested against the real binary (on Linux); the Windows run, driver DPC behaviour and your card's thermals are not. The crash diagnosis is from forum reports, not your logs.
- **Could fail if:** a GPU job starts outside llama-swap (Ollama or ComfyUI launched by hand): the guard's `--stop` runs once per set and can't stop a later reload, so Ollama goes inside llama-swap too (Day 3); the guard misses Serato's process name (I couldn't confirm the exact `.exe`: run `dj_guard.py --once` with Serato open); or the crashes are PSU transients that no clock cap stops.
- **First step:** install llama-swap with [`prototypes/llama-swap.example.yaml`](prototypes/llama-swap.example.yaml) as the starting config, run [`prototypes/dj_guard.py`](prototypes/dj_guard.py), move one workload (the VLM) behind it, and log `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=power.draw,clocks.gr,temperature.gpu,memory.used --format=csv -l 1` plus HWiNFO memory-junction temperature during the next long Reel Brain batch.

### 4. Fix Reel Brain's looping JSON at the source: image-token budget, sampling, quant, bounded schema
- **Why:** two open issues match your stack closely. Ollama [#13084](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/13084): Qwen3-VL 4B/8B on **Windows with an RTX 3080** produces "highly unsorted contents" or repeats short sentences once the prompt exceeds `num_ctx`; the reporter's images were 3236×2160 and the logs said "truncating input prompt". [QwenLM/Qwen3-VL#1611](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL/issues/1611): Qwen3-VL loops across runtimes (Transformers, Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp), more with **greedy decoding** and **low (4-bit) quants**; the same author ran GLM-4.6V-Flash on 500 cases with zero loops. A Qwen-VL image costs roughly `h × w / (32 × 32)` tokens, so a full-resolution 1080×1920 frame is ~2,000 tokens and eight of them ~16,000. **And on a card under 24 GB, Ollama's default context is 4K tokens** ([Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/context-length)): unless Reel Brain sets `num_ctx` on every request (its code isn't on GitHub, so I couldn't check), three full-resolution frames already overflow it, which is exactly the truncation in #13084. Check this first.
- **Do:** (1) downscale frames to ≤ 640 px on the long side (a 360×640 frame is ~225 tokens by the formula above), cap at 6–8 per reel, set `num_ctx` explicitly and log prompt tokens per request; (2) use Qwen's recommended Instruct sampling (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, presence penalty 1.5), not temperature 0; (3) Q6_K at 8K context (~9 GB) instead of the 4-bit build (by its 6.1 GB size, Ollama's `qwen3-vl:8b` is 4-bit); Q8 with its projector and cache passes 12 GB (arithmetic in the YAML); (4) a schema that cannot run away: `maxItems` on every array, `maxLength` on **every** string including each list item, enums, thinking off. Tested on today's llama.cpp converter (what llama-server and Ollama 0.30+ use): every bound becomes a grammar limit, but Pydantic's `list[str]` items come out *unbounded*, so one "takeaway" can loop to the token limit; the guard bounds them. Set the output cap from the schema's worst case (~2,200 tokens here, counting Arabic at ~2 characters a token); the guard's old cap of 700 would have cut long valid notes off mid-JSON. Validate with Pydantic, retry once, then fall back to "free text → a second small call that only formats JSON"; (5) A/B GLM-4.6V-Flash (9B, MIT per model listings, GGUF available) on the same 20 reels.
- **Ollama itself:** the bug where it ignored repetition penalties ([#15783](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/15783)) was closed in July 2026 as obsoleted by 0.30's move onto llama.cpp, but the umbrella issue [#14493](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14493) is still open and nobody has confirmed penalties work on 0.30+. Check `ollama --version` (update if older than 0.30), diagnose on Ollama (Day 2), rerun the same reels on llama-server behind llama-swap (Day 3), and keep whichever loops less.
- **Confidence: medium.** The issues match your hardware and model; nobody has run your prompts.
- **Could fail if:** the prompts themselves invite open-ended lists or the model needs thinking to read the frames. The bounded schema and the GLM A/B cover those.
- **First step:** record your Ollama version and the prompt-token count for the 20 reels that failed most recently, then rerun them with downscaled frames and the recommended sampling. [`prototypes/vlm_json_guard.py`](prototypes/vlm_json_guard.py) does all of that against Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible server.

### 7. Move Hermes to llama.cpp with expert offload, and cut the tool list by hand
- **Why:** Hermes' own local-runtime design is the right shape for 12 GB: MoE expert weights in system RAM, attention and KV cache on the GPU, ≥64K context ([local-models docs](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/blob/main/website/docs/user-guide/local-models.md)). Your 3080 Ti's extra bandwidth helps less than it seems: the offloaded experts run at your RAM's speed. InsiderLLM measured only up to 8K tokens of context; I found no speed for a 64K window holding Hermes' ~13–15k-token opening prompt (below).
- **Prompt size:** Hermes' guide says prefilling the fixed prompt and tool schemas "dominates the first turn" on low-VRAM setups, and behind llama-swap every swap brings that first turn back, so shrink the fixed prompt first: `hermes prompt-size` shows it (on a fresh install of today's `main`: a 6.8 KB system prompt plus 46.9 KB of tool schemas for 22 default tools, roughly 13–15k tokens before any MCP server of yours), and `hermes tools` and per-profile toolsets cut it.

Your current Qwen3-Coder-30B on Ollama uses a different renderer, so it stays as the fallback.

- **Known Qwen3.6 + llama.cpp issues in Hermes, with workarounds:** pass `--chat-template-file` with the right Qwen template, not only `--jinja`, or output can be garbage ([#29677](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/29677)); leave `capabilities.answer_in_reasoning` unset, because a tool call stranded inside `<think>` still happened in **1 of 23 tool-call turns** ([#129440](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/129440)); `search_files` can loop when the model passes a glob as a regex ([#66129](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/66129)). Expect a few percent of turns to need a retry: "reliable" at 12 GB means "recovers well", not "never fails".

Qwen3.5-9B (Apache-2.0, dense, fully on the GPU) with 16 GB. The challenger: **Gemma 4 12B** (Apache-2.0, native function calling, 256K context, a dense size added in June 2026 per secondary write-ups; [ggml-org GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/gemma-4-12B-GGUF)). No published tool-calling scores for the 12B, so the replay decides.

You have better information. **Serato DJ 4 logs every load** in `master.sqlite` → `history_entry` (artist, title, BPM, key, deck, `start_time`, `played`; verified in the [whats-now-playing reader](https://github.com/whatsnowplaying/whats-now-playing/blob/main/nowplaying/serato/reader.py)). Your library files carry Serato's beat grid (a `Serato BeatGrid` tag inside the file). Align each played track to the set recording (your cross-correlation code plus whatstheID's speed ladder), then transform that track's grid into set time. This is the published "DJ-mix reverse engineering" approach (Schwarz & Fourer; Kim et al., ISMIR 2020).
- **Fallback:** Beat This ([CPJKU/beat_this](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this), MIT, small checkpoints, pip install, no madmom needed) for tracks you don't own. Evidence it suits your genre: a user benchmark on Giant Steps (664 Beatport electronic tracks) in [issue #13](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/13) got **89.3 % strict / 95.0 % octave-tolerant** tempo accuracy, 90.9 / 96.4 % with an ensemble of the *small* models (which did better than the big ones on near-constant tempo). The maintainers confirmed in [issue #16](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/16) that the MIT licence covers the weights. Add an octave-correction prior (120–160 BPM for techno).
- **Recording note:** the MC4000 unlocks **Serato DJ Lite**, which has a History panel but no built-in mix recorder (Pro has one). If your recordings come from OBS or the controller, alignment is the same; only the clock you seed it with differs. On Serato 3.x the history is the older binary `_Serato_/History/Sessions/*.session` format (also parsed by whats-now-playing) instead of `master.sqlite`.
- **Precedent on real mixes:** Kim et al. (ISMIR 2020, [djmix-analysis](https://github.com/mir-aidj/djmix-analysis)) aligned tracks to 1,557 real 1001Tracklists mixes with subsequence DTW (it finds where each track sits in the mix, allowing for tempo changes), worked example a Boiler Room set; they report DJs rarely change tempo or key much, which keeps stretch ratios small. Their repo has no licence file: read it, don't copy it.
- **Free with it: track-ID captions.** The same history names every track and when it came in, so [`prototypes/track_id_captions.py`](prototypes/track_id_captions.py) writes an "ID: Artist – Title" SRT for any clip window (the first question under every club clip), and the end-to-end test burns it in through FilmCraft.
- **Confidence: medium.** The history fields are verified from code; alignment through heavy EQ/filter/FX is not tested on your sets.
- **Could fail if:** the DJ rides the pitch so the stretch ratio drifts inside a track (fit it piecewise) or the track was played from a streaming service (no file).
- **First step:** list the last session with [`prototypes/serato_set_grid.py`](prototypes/serato_set_grid.py) `history`, then `align` three tracks you own against the recording. In a synthetic mix (track 3 % fast, bass cut for a 30 s blend, crowd noise) it recovered the ratio exactly and the offset to 15 ms.

### 9. Reframe from body pose + an L1-optimal camera path
- **Why:** YuNet finds faces; a DJ looks down at the controller under strobes, so faces drop out exactly when it matters. A real operator frames the body and hands, holds still, then pans deliberately.
- **Do:** detect people and keypoints with RTMPose via [rtmlib](https://github.com/Tau-J/rtmlib) (Apache-2.0, ONNX, light) and keep YuNet as one signal. Choose the subject per shot, then solve the crop path offline as an L1-optimal path (Grundmann et al., "Auto-Directed Video Stabilization with Robust L1 Optimal Camera Paths", the method behind YouTube's stabiliser): the L1 objective produces static holds, constant pans and smooth ease-ins, like a camera operator. Google's AutoFlip (polynomial L2 fit) is the obvious off-the-shelf alternative, but MediaPipe lists it as a legacy solution whose support ended on 2023-03-01. I found MATLAB re-implementations of the L1 paper but no maintained Python one; the linear program is small enough to write with `scipy.optimize.linprog` (SciPy BSD-3, its HiGHS solver MIT) or cvxpy (Apache-2.0).
- **If pose drops out in strobes:** [SAM 3.1](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3) (March 2026) tracks every instance of a short text prompt through a video ("person behind the DJ decks"), 848M parameters, CUDA ≥ 12.6. Licence: Meta's **SAM License**, royalty-free and commercial use allowed, but custom: trade-control terms, the licence ends if you sue Meta over IP, and anything you redistribute stays under it. Call it from Creative Studio; never put it in the MIT/Apache fork. Windows support isn't stated; test it on one clip before relying on it.
- **Avoid:** Ultralytics YOLO (AGPL-3.0) anywhere near the FilmCraft fork.
- **Into FilmCraft, tested:** Motion's `anchor` is in source pixels, so the solved path goes in as anchor keyframes at the path's corners (the path simplified to the fewest points within 3 px). [`prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py`](prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py) does it against a real build of your fork: on a subject sweeping across the frame with noisy, gappy detections, the subject stayed fully in the 9:16 frame on 100 % of reframed frames (a centre crop: 34 %) with 8–14 keyframes per 3-second shot. **Pitfall the test caught:** the L1 path keeps the subject's *centre* inside its safe zone, so the margin must be at least half the subject's width (a DJ's body, not a face) or the edges leave the frame; at the old 60 px margin only 91 % of frames kept the whole subject.
- **Confidence: medium.** The path and the FilmCraft side are tested on synthetic footage; detection quality on dark, strobing club footage is not.
- **Could fail if:** body detection drops out in strobe and darkness as often as faces do (then a fixed, slightly wide crop on the booth beats any tracking, which #6's "selection, not cutting" hypothesis would also favour), or the subject isn't one person (B2B sets: frame the pair, not the louder one).
- **First step:** run rtmlib on one 60-second DJ clip and plot face-detection vs body-detection coverage per frame; feed the body centres to [`prototypes/l1_reframe_path.py`](prototypes/l1_reframe_path.py). On a synthetic swaying DJ with 15 px jitter and 10 % missed detections, its path held still 84 % of the time with no direction changes, where a moving-average follow changed direction 189 times.

### 10. Write cue points into Serato, and grids only where Serato's are wrong
- **Why:** Serato analyses a grid on import, and your tempo already matches Serato's to 0.01 BPM on 17 tracks, so rewriting grids buys little. The value is (a) **cue points Serato never sets**: `analyze.py` already finds intro end, drop and outro start, prep you do by hand today; and (b) **locked grids where the two disagree** and your fit is clean (your notes have one: "Twice or it's Luck", Serato 144 vs your 140). [rb2serato](https://github.com/SM4RTENHEIMER/rb2serato) (MIT, Sep 2026) has encoders for `Serato Markers2`, `Serato Markers_` and `Serato BeatGrid` that it says match Serato DJ Pro 2.x–4.x byte for byte, and documents Serato 4's import quirks (crate header exactly `1.0/Serato ScratchLive Crate`, WAV typed `wave`, Serato closed while writing).
- **What it doesn't do (read in its code):** FLAC, the format of 89 of the 90 entries in your repo's `library.json` (some may point at deleted files) (rb2serato writes ID3 and M4A, serato-tools only MP3/AIFF; Serato keeps FLAC data in Vorbis comments `SERATO_BEATGRID` / `SERATO_MARKERS_V2` per [Holzhaus/serato-tags](https://github.com/Holzhaus/serato-tags)); a grid anchor (whatstheID stores the BPM and no beat position, `_comb_tempo` finds the phase and throws it away, and 0.01 BPM off is ~27 ms of drift by the end of a six-minute track); Windows (its writer shells out to macOS `cp -c`). And cues share one tag with your hand-set cues, loops, track colour and BPM lock, so a naive write wipes your prep. And on a recorded loop your `analyze.py` gets the tempo right (124.99) but puts cues ~25 ms after the kick (drop cue 13.47 s, kick 13.443 s), because they snap to librosa's beats; as hot cues they would start just after the attack.
- **Prototype ([`serato_writeback.py`](prototypes/serato_writeback.py), tested):** snaps cues onto the fitted grid (a cue more than 80 ms from any line is left where it is), merges them into free slots (skips one within 250 ms of yours; keeps loops, colour and lock; refuses Serato Flip data it can't re-encode); fits anchor and BPM to every kick transient across the track and refuses a drifting tempo; writes MP3/WAV/AIFF and FLAC by copy → tag → audio-hash check → replace. On a recorded 125 BPM loop the anchor landed 0.9 ms from the kick (124.9997 BPM; librosa's `beat_track` said 123.05, your own lag-grid finding); 180 s of synthetic techno at 132.004 BPM analysed as 132.0 came back 132.0042. Tags read back identically through its decoder and through serato-tools (MP3, FLAC), audio bytes unchanged. Its write path imports mutagen (GPL) at run time, so after the first test move it into whatstheID and delete it from the fork.
- **MP3 timing:** Serato's MP3 timeline differs from FFmpeg/libmad decoding by ~24–26 ms (Mixxx corrects Serato cues by −26 ms at 44.1 kHz and −24 ms at 48 kHz; GPL, read for the numbers only); FLAC and WAV don't. Measure yours with `compare` on MP3s Serato analysed and pass `--offset-ms`.
- **Confidence: medium.** Two independent parsers agree on the bytes and the anchor is exact on one recorded loop, but nothing has been loaded by Serato; rb2serato has 1 star; the FLAC layout predates Serato 4. Serato 4 keeps its library in SQLite and re-imports `database V2` and crates at start-up when they change; nobody documents whether it re-reads rewritten tags on a track it already knows, which is exactly your case.
- **Could fail if:** Serato keeps its cached grid for known tracks (then each write needs a re-import), Serato 4 reads FLAC Serato fields differently from the pre-4 notes, or your MP3 offset varies by encoder (measure it per source: your downloads vs your bought files).
- **First step:** apply the small `analyze.py` patch that keeps the comb's beat phase (`whatstheid-beat-phase.diff`, sent separately because whatstheID is private; tested on the recorded loop). Then back up `_Serato_` and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Serato`; with Serato closed, run `serato_writeback.py write --cues-only` with whatstheID's cues on **copies** of three files Serato has never seen (one MP3, one FLAC, one WAV) and on one FLAC it already knows and you've set cues on; reopen Serato and check the new cues land where written and yours are untouched. Then try `anchor` + a grid write on the one track where Serato's BPM is wrong. If the known track doesn't show the new cues, the write-back needs a re-import step, which changes the 2–3 day estimate; if the fresh FLAC shows no grid, the FLAC layout is wrong and FLAC waits.

**House look per client (not in the Top 10: still low confidence, because taste can't be checked without you)**
- **Why:** "stock AI" is mostly the absence of decisions: default type, eased slides, a new colour per scene. A look written down as rules can be checked by your existing motion-graphics QA step; taste that lives in a prompt cannot.
- **Do:** per client, one `look.md` (also the Inspiration Canvas format, 2.5): one display face and one text face from libre foundries (Velvetyne: OFL or GPL per font; Collletttivo: free for commercial use; Use & Modify: a curated list, check each), never changed mid-series; a type scale; a palette sampled from *their* footage (dominant colours + one accent); and a **motion grammar on the bar grid**: things appear and hold on downbeats, move only across bar lines, hard-cut on the drop, no ease-in-out slides. Use [motionmaxxing](https://github.com/Tejashmakwana/motionmaxxing) (Apache-2.0) as the rubric: its tells ("everything is a slide", typed-out headlines with a caret, a new flat colour per scene) and counter-rules come from 53 frame-accurate professional motion moments, where pros used **3,392 transform keyframes against 584 opacity fades** (things move and cut; they rarely fade), though on brand films, not club footage.
- **The rhythm half is checkable now:** [`prototypes/beat_lint.py`](prototypes/beat_lint.py) lints a HyperFrames composition against the track's grid (clip starts and ends on beats, titles marked `data-beat="bar"` on bar lines, relative starts resolved, GSAP tween positions on beats) and flags in-out eased moves and typed-out text; exit code 1 means a finding. It follows sub-compositions (`data-composition-src`, how HyperFrames projects are split into files) on the absolute timeline. **Its limit, found on HyperFrames' own chat example:** that example computes every word's time in a JS loop (`startTime + words.length * 0.2`), and a static linter cannot read those. It now reports them as `unchecked_positions` (10 there) rather than passing them, and it caught the one literal it could read (a tween at 14.0 s, 62 ms off a 128 BPM beat). So: have the agent write times as beat counts in code (`beat(n)` = n × 60/bpm), or check the rendered video instead.
- **Confidence: low.** Taste is the least verifiable claim here. To raise it, show the collective two versions of the same clip (current graphics vs a `look.md` version that passes the lint) blind, and count which they'd post; if the house look wins, it belongs in the Top 10.
- **Could fail if:** the rules are followed and the result is merely tidy. The reference measurements (Top 10 #6) and your eye on every first clip of a series are the backstop.
- **First step:** write `look.md` for the collective you want to pitch, using their existing flyers as the source.

**Shot picking from two synced angles (prototype: [`shot_picker.py`](prototypes/shot_picker.py), tested).** Per bar and angle it scores sharpness (Laplacian variance), exposure, motion and strobe flashes (pose, hands-up and taste scores can be added per bar), then picks an angle per bar by dynamic programming: cuts only on bar lines, each shot at least 2 bars and preferably ≤ 4 (a soft limit, so a shot runs long only when the other angle is unusable), crowd angle on the drop bar and the next (the reaction lags the drop). It writes a plan for `plan_to_filmcraft.py`. In its test (two synthetic angles, one dark for 4 bars, the other out of focus for 6) it avoided both bad stretches and put the crowd on the drop; a hard 4-bar maximum had forced a cut to the blurred angle, which is why the maximum is a cost, not a wall. Take the limits from your Top 10 #6 medians, not my defaults. Later, learn your taste: ~200 keep/skip labels and a logistic head on frozen SigLIP embeddings (Apache-2.0); an IEEE paper on multi-camera editing learned an editor's preferences from 33 scenes with CLIP + SVM. The LLM only chooses among pre-scored candidates; it never watches video.

**Planner and judge in 12 GB**

- **Don't let a model judge taste.** Timeline-Bench (arXiv 2609.35143, Sep 2026) gave agents 56 realistic raw-footage editing tasks: the best resolved 26.8 % (15 of 56; 95 % interval 15.8–40.3 %), the average 14 %, and 562 of 771 failed runs failed *only* the quality test (calibrated on 2,582 blind judgments by 43 editors), which the authors put down to agents judging footage from stills and transcripts instead of watching it. Pairwise LLM judges still flip with presentation order in up to 20 % of cases (J1, 2025). **Router (now EmbeddingGemma, your change)** — see Top 10 #3.
- Labels have to come from outside the router: your corrections (a "wrong route" button in Telegram), implicit signals (you re-ask, you switch model by hand, the task fails), and a teacher model labelling logged messages overnight. Fine-tuning on Laya's own logged guesses teaches it nothing.
- Route by calibrated `answer_confidence`; below the threshold, fall back to the main LLM and queue the message for labelling.
- If your router is nearest-example lookup, try a logistic-regression head on the same embeddings once you have ~20 labelled messages per route: it usually beats lookup and still runs in milliseconds (`laya_eval.py --embed` compares them).
- Question design: one coarse question (which agent/tool family) then a fine one, ≤ ~20 options each, opaque labels (`A`/`B`) rather than yes/no words.
- **Give the route a job that saves GPU time.** Hermes plugins can't switch the model per turn: `pre_llm_call` can only inject context and `pre_tool_call` can only block or approve (Hermes plugin docs). So a route has to act *in front of* Hermes. The payoff that matters on 12 GB: send each message to a Hermes profile with only the toolset that route needs (vault, media, coding, chat; `hermes profile`, per-profile toolsets). Prefill of the fixed prompt dominates the first turn after every swap (Top 10 #7), so a vault message answered by a profile with five vault tools instead of every MCP server is faster on the same model. Measure it with `hermes prompt-size` per profile.
- **Hermes' side-jobs also hit the GPU.** Compression, vision, approval scoring (the `smart` approvals mode), MCP tool routing, session titles, skill search and the curator are "auxiliary" model slots that default to the main model. Leave them on `auto` (the already-loaded model, so nothing swaps); point `auxiliary.vision` at the agent model only if it has its projector loaded, otherwise every image swaps the VLM in.

**Classification and repo linking**
- **Why:** kNN at 63 % is a weak baseline. A logistic-regression "linear probe" on the same embeddings often beats kNN once there are enough labels per folder (measure it below); showing the top 3 turns a 63 % top-1 into a much higher hit rate for one click. For repos, a published study (SONAD) found LLMs over-accept candidates (precision 68 %, recall 97 %) while a feature-based verifier reached 82 % precision.
- **Measure before changing anything:** [`prototypes/collections_probe.py`](prototypes/collections_probe.py) takes the embeddings and folder labels you already have and reports, under the same 5-fold cross-validation, kNN (k = 1/5/15), a logistic-regression probe, the probe's top-3, an "ask me when unsure" rule (how many it sends you, how accurate the rest are), per-folder accuracy and the most-confused folder pairs. On synthetic embeddings with two overlapping folders it showed the expected shape (kNN 0.75–0.81, probe 0.86, top-3 0.999; the rule sent 13 % to you and was 91 % right on the rest), but that is synthetic; your 63 % is the baseline to beat.
- **Do:** (a) re-label 50 random reels blind to find your own consistency ceiling; (b) linear probe + top-3 + LLM rerank only when the top-2 margin is small; (c) for repos, three tiers ending in "no repo" (many tools in reels are closed SaaS products): the prototype below. Your whatstheID `verify.py` already encodes the right rules (empty is a refusal, exact-match preference, word-level coverage × precision).
- **Borrow ideas from:** [juspay/dopamine](https://github.com/juspay/dopamine) (MIT, active, but **1 star**: a fresh repo, not a proven tool), the same pipeline on Gemini: a link resolver that remembers failed lookups, a verifier, and a project mapper (embedding prefilter + judge with an "avoid" field per project), which is the shape your per-project ranking needs.
- **Prototype:** [`prototypes/repo_linker.py`](prototypes/repo_linker.py) implements the three tiers (explicit `github.com/owner/repo`, `owner/repo` and Hugging Face `org/model` strings in the OCR text, caption and transcript, checked to exist, since tech reels often show the repo page; search candidates that must match the name exactly after normalising, then ranked by how much of the repo's description the reel's own context covers, owner, stars, fork/archived penalties; "no repo" and "ambiguous" outcomes). Not run against the live GitHub API from here (blocked); unauthenticated search allows ~10 requests a minute, so set `GITHUB_TOKEN` and cache results for the 1,000-reel backlog.
- **Confidence: medium.** The logic is tested on constructed cases; precision on your real reels is unmeasured.
- **First step:** the 50-item blind re-label, then run the linker on the same 50 reels and count false links before and after.

Claps alone can only tell {1, 3} from {2, 4}, never beat 1 from beat 3, so a loop with no structural change comes back `half_bar_ambiguous` instead of a guess. Phrase starts count in 8-bar steps from the track's first bar, with novelty as a second opinion flagged when it disagrees: on a recorded loop, "the bar after the biggest change" was a bar late, because a crash on a phrase's first bar and a fill on its last both make one bar stand out. Synthetic 150 BPM techno: right phase in 6 of 6 seeds with structure, claps-only always flagged; the recorded 125 BPM loop: phase 0, phrases at 0 and 15.36 s. Full tracks untested. ## 5. What I got wrong along the way

The biggest calls the audits killed or reversed (details and dates in `audit-log.md`):

1. **"Ollama ignores repetition penalties, so switch servers to stop the looping JSON."** True before 0.30; the bug was then closed as obsoleted by 0.30's llama.cpp engine, not confirmed fixed. The likelier causes on your exact stack are image tokens overflowing `num_ctx` (an open issue reproduced on an RTX 3080 under Windows), greedy decoding and 4-bit quants. *Watch for:* bug reports age fast; check the closing comment, not just the title.
2. **"Turn on Hermes Tool Search to shrink the prompt."** It breaks Qwen models (Top 10 #7). *Watch for:* features that work with the vendor's default model and not with yours.
3. **"Let a local VLM judge rank the three variants."** Agents judging edits from stills fail mostly on craft (Timeline-Bench), and pairwise judges flip with presentation order. Demoted to a defect checker. *Watch for:* using a model to grade taste.
4. **"Write a GPU broker", then "free ComfyUI through llama-swap when Serato starts".** llama-swap already does the brokering, ComfyUI included. And my DJ guard's ComfyUI call went through llama-swap's `/comfyui/` route, which *starts* ComfyUI: in a test against the real binary, DJ mode ended with ComfyUI on the GPU. The guard now uses a `dj` profile plus re-unloading. *Watch for:* building infrastructure before searching for it, and trusting glue code you haven't run against the real thing.
5. **"Replace Laya with embeddings + logistic regression", then "fine-tune it for fewer epochs on small data".** The first was written before I knew Laya is built to be fine-tuned (the real problem is that it runs zero-shot). The second was backwards: Laya's own issue #963 shows the 4-epoch default gives a small dataset too few updates and the head collapses to the class prior; small data needs *more* epochs. *Watch for:* advice about a component nobody has looked at, and paraphrasing an issue instead of reading its numbers.
6. **"rb2serato already did the risky half of the Serato write-back."** It writes no FLAC, runs only on macOS as written and needs a grid anchor whatstheID doesn't store, and the value was cues, not grids (Top 10 #10). My own replacement then went wrong the same way: its kick detector was exact on synthetic kicks and 23 ms late on a recorded one. *Watch for:* a README standing in for reading the code against your own files, and synthetic tests standing in for real audio.
7. **"The collective is the first income."** Local reel editing goes for as little as 100 EGP; it is a portfolio play. The dollars are in Arabic-dialect clips for remote clients.
8. **"Qwen3.6 runs at ~36 tok/s on a 12 GB card", then "up to 47 tok/s at `--n-cpu-moe 18`".** The first was untraceable; the second used a setting that can't hold Q4_K_M experts in 12 GB, and my example config's `--n-cpu-moe 22` would probably have run out of VRAM too (the traceable 38.9 tok/s was also measured on a headless card, which yours isn't). Same family of error later: a "~90 tok/s" for Gemma 4 12B that no source supports, and a $0.40/hour Scribe price that had fallen to $0.22. *Watch for:* numbers without the quant, context, hardware and date they were measured at.
9. **"FilmCraft can't do Arabic; set Arabic in the HTML layer."** Based on an upstream bug title. I built the fork and burned an Egyptian caption into an export: with the caption font set to an Arabic face it renders correctly; only the default font fails. (I then made the same mistake in the same area: "FilmCraft drops VP9 alpha, so use ProRes 4444", from skimming its Matroska code; tested, both formats composite correctly.) *Watch for:* bug titles standing in for a test.
10. **"QwenCleo-ASR covers the Egyptian-Arabic caption job."** It covers the words, not their timing: Qwen's forced aligner skips Arabic, and the obvious substitute's default model is non-commercial. Word-by-word captions need a second, commercially licensed aligner. *Watch for:* a benchmark on the easy half of a job (WER) standing in for the whole job (timed captions).

- **Hermes + Laya (now moot: you replaced Laya with EmbeddingGemma): drop the router entirely and let the main model route.** *For:* zero-shot Laya is near chance by its own README; Qwen3.6 at ~40 tok/s can classify a message in a second or two and is probably more accurate than an untuned 400M encoder; one less moving part. *Against:* every message would wake the GPU model (contention with ComfyUI and Reel Brain), and Laya is free to run on CPU. *Verdict:* it wins **if you won't label 2–3k messages**. If you will, the fine-tuned Laya is cheaper per message and keeps the GPU free.
- **Reel Brain: send the backlog to a cloud video model.** *For:* your saved reels are public content, so privacy is not the usual objection; a hosted Gemini-class model reads video and audio natively, returns schema-constrained JSON, and takes the 1,000-reel backlog off your GPU entirely (no loops on your card, no crashes). Dopamine does exactly this. *Against:* Egypt is on Google's official [list of regions for the Gemini API and AI Studio](https://ai.google.dev/available_regions), but I could not confirm the current free-tier video limits; free-tier inputs may be used by Google to improve its products (fine for public reels, not for anything private); paying needs a card that works abroad; it breaks your local-first rule. *Verdict:* worth one afternoon's test on 50 reels (AI Studio is offered in Egypt); keep the local pipeline for new saves.
- **whatstheID: pay AudD for long mixes instead of relying on shazamio.** *For:* shazamio is an unofficial Shazam client that can break any day; AudD is a paid, documented API, and `recognize.py` already falls back to its standard endpoint (about 12 s per request; $5 per 1,000 requests after 300 free, less at volume). Its enterprise endpoint takes hours-long files and bills per 12 s of audio, with a `skip`/`every` option to sample one 12 s chunk every 2 minutes: 60 units a two-hour mix, **~$0.30 at the standard rate** (enterprise prices are not published). It named 3 of your 6 underground test records (2.4). *Against:* paid, card from Egypt, and your library fingerprinter still beats both on records you own. *Verdict:* **it wins as the cloud fallback** behind your own fingerprinter. When you next touch `recognize.py`, drop shazamio and move the existing AudD call to the enterprise endpoint for long files.
- **Inspiration Canvas: build it, on tldraw's agent starter kit.** *For:* tldraw ships an MIT agent starter kit (`npm create tldraw@latest -- --template agent`) where a model reads the canvas (screenshots plus shape data) and edits it through validated actions; that is "Claude reads my taste board" out of the box, and the SDK runs without a licence key on `localhost`. *Against:* it is a new app to maintain, and Obsidian Canvas + Eagle already cover collecting and reading. *Verdict:* still no, for now. If the vault-plus-Eagle setup proves too static after a month of use, this is the starting point.
