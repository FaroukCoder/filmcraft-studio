# Projects research: what to build, adopt or drop

**Status:** the current best report after 37 rounds of research and hostile audit; `audit-log.md` has what each round attacked, broke and changed. The scripts in [`prototypes/`](prototypes/README.md) were run against their tests here (Linux), not only written; none has run on your Windows PC yet.
**Written for:** a solo dev and techno DJ in Cairo with one Windows 10 PC (RTX 3080 Ti, 12 GB VRAM, 300 W cap), local-first.
**Date of research:** 2026-10-14. Repo stats (stars, last commit, licence) were checked on that date.

> One unknown changes several answers: **how much system RAM you have.** Mixture-of-experts models with
> expert offload (the fastest way to run a 30B-class model on 12 GB) keep ~11–13 GB of weights in RAM (24–26 of
> Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's 40 layers of experts at ~0.49 GB each at Q4_K_M): 32 GB is
> comfortable, 16 GB has been reported to work with nothing else open. Wherever that matters, both options are given.

## The blunt version

- **Creative Studio is the right top priority, and the LLM is not where its quality comes from.** In a September 2026 benchmark of agents editing raw footage, the best resolved 27 % of tasks, failing mostly on craft. Your edge is signal (beats, drops, phrases, pose, Serato's own logs) plus your taste. Use the model to fill in a plan, not to judge one. The plumbing works: I built your fork and drove it headless from a JSON plan to a finished 9:16 clip with cuts, a reframe and burned-in Arabic captions (Top 10 #5).
- **Laya, as shipped, is close to a coin toss.** Its own README says the base checkpoints are near chance zero-shot. Fine-tune and calibrate it on your labels, or take it out of the path.
- **A 12 GB local agent will not be as reliable as Claude Code.** Expect a few percent of tool turns to need a retry. Use Hermes for routing, automation and vault work; keep heavy coding on Claude Code.
- **Don't build the Inspiration Canvas app.** Store boards as JSON Canvas in your vault (or buy Eagle for $35) and spend the time on Creative Studio.
- **Don't take whatstheID public.** The "paste a mix, get a tracklist" space is crowded and the download features carry legal risk. Finish the Serato cue write-back (Top 10 #10); it is the part nobody else does.
- **Generated footage (MiniMax H3) doesn't belong in paid club clips:** the product is that it was real. **Qwen-Image 2.1 doesn't belong in anything you sell** (non-commercial licence, Top 10 #1); flyers and announcement stills from FLUX.2 klein 4B are fine.
- **The GPU rule is enforceable today, not a habit.** llama-swap plus a small guard makes every model answer "not now" while Serato or Ableton runs; tested against the real llama-swap (Top 10 #2), which is how a version that would have *loaded* ComfyUI mid-set got caught.
- **Five projects plus a FilmCraft fork is too many for one person.** The first week touches Creative Studio, Hermes/Laya, the shared GPU setup, and Reel Brain's looping JSON only because it shares the GPU and the vision model; whatstheID and the canvas wait.

### 1. Take client-facing image generation off Qwen-Image 2.1
- **Why:** the official [Qwen-Image-2.1 model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1) says "This model is licensed under the Qwen Research License Agreement", and its [LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE) grants use "FOR NON-COMMERCIAL PURPOSES ONLY"; commercial use needs a separate licence from Qwen (model-business@notice.qwencloud.com). The 2.1-Turbo and prompt-rewriter (PE) checkpoints carry the same licence. The original Qwen-Image was Apache-2.0; 2.1 is not. Anything you sell (the collective's clips, flyers) cannot use it.
- **Instead:** FLUX.2 [klein] **4B** (Apache-2.0; the 9B is non-commercial) or Z-Image-Turbo (Apache-2.0, 6B, 8 steps). Both fit 12 GB quantized. If you want to stay in the Qwen family, Qwen-Image-2512 (Dec 2025) was released under Apache-2.0 per the [Qwen-Image repo](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image) (heavier: ~20B). Keep Qwen-Image 2.1 for personal experiments only.
- **Confidence: high.** The licence text is quoted from the official card and LICENSE file (read through search-engine copies; Hugging Face itself is blocked from my tools), and every secondary source agrees.
- **Could fail if:** Qwen re-licenses (it happened in the other direction before) or your outputs are judged "not commercial". Unlikely to matter: switching costs hours.
- **First step:** build one ComfyUI workflow on FLUX.2 klein 4B (Day 7) and rerun 10 prompts from your direction folder; keep Qwen-Image 2.1 for private experiments only.

### 2. Put every GPU server behind llama-swap, plus a small DJ-mode guard
- **Why:** every project competes for one 12 GB GPU and the PC has crashed on long runs. You do not need to write a broker: [llama-swap](https://github.com/mostlygeek/llama-swap) (MIT, active, WinGet package for Windows) already starts and stops GPU servers on demand and keeps only one group loaded. It handles llama-server (LLM and VLM), whisper.cpp, stable-diffusion.cpp, audio.cpp and **ComfyUI** (a dedicated `/comfyui/` endpoint and a reserved `comfyui_auto` model ID so the browser websocket doesn't pin the GPU), plus `/upstream/<model>/` passthrough for anything else HTTP. `groups` with `exclusive: true` give the "only one big thing at a time" rule; `POST /api/models/unload` empties the card; `ttl` frees idle models.
- **Do:** (1) define each GPU workload as a llama-swap model (agent LLM, VLM, ASR, ComfyUI) in one exclusive group; (2) point Hermes (`providers.llamacpp.base_url`), Reel Brain, Creative Studio and ComfyUI clients at llama-swap instead of their own servers; (3) a small **DJ guard** that polls for `Serato DJ Pro.exe` / `Serato DJ Lite.exe` / `Ableton Live*.exe` (psutil) and, while they run, switches llama-swap to a `dj` profile whose pins are all null (every model answers 404 instead of loading: `PUT /api/profiles/active`), unloads everything (`POST /api/models/unload`), and re-unloads anything that starts anyway, because llama-swap's `/comfyui/` route ignores profiles; on exit it clears the profile. No service restarts needed; (4) make every batch job checkpoint per item and retry on connection refused, so the guard costs a pause, not a lost run: [`prototypes/gpu_queue.py`](prototypes/gpu_queue.py) is that queue (SQLite in WAL mode, one row per item, waits on the DJ lock, retries with backoff, parks repeat failures; its test kills a worker mid-item and the restart redoes only that item). Hermes cron's no-agent mode can drain the queue at night.
- **Trade-off:** Hermes' managed runtime sizes context and expert offload for you; behind llama-swap you set `--n-cpu-moe`, `-c` and the chat template yourself in the YAML. That is ten lines, once.
- **Why the DJ guard matters beyond VRAM:** NVIDIA driver DPC-latency spikes of 3–10 ms are a known cause of audio dropouts on Windows; heavy CUDA work makes it worse. Run LatencyMon once with a GPU job active and once without, while Serato plays.
- **Hardware side:** on RTX 30-series, crashes under load are often millisecond transients that a power cap does not stop. Cheap things to try: cap the core clock (`nvidia-smi -lgc 210,1700` as admin locks it between 210 and 1,700 MHz; pick the upper number a little under the peak `clocks.gr` your own log shows, or use an Afterburner curve), and watch **GDDR6X memory junction temperature** in HWiNFO during a long LLM or Whisper run (memory-bound work behaves like mining, and forum reports put 3080-class junctions past 100 °C there; [one pad mod](https://www.igorslab.de/en/simple-pad-mod-for-the-force-rtx-3080-founders-edition-slowers-the-gddr6x-temperature-by-a-whopping-8-degrees/) took 8 °C off). If junction temps are high, thermal pads; if crashes persist at a capped clock, suspect the PSU.
- **Tested:** I built llama-swap from source and ran the committed config with stand-in servers: asking for `vlm` unloads `agent` (one GPU owner), `/comfyui/` loads ComfyUI, transcription requests reach the QwenCleo entry, and the guard's DJ mode returns 404 for every model, empties the card, unloads a ComfyUI job that sneaks in, and restores service afterwards ([`test_dj_guard_llama_swap.py`](prototypes/test_dj_guard_llama_swap.py)). (Its first version would have loaded ComfyUI during your set; 5, item 4.)
- **Confidence: medium.** The mechanics are tested against the real binary (on Linux); the Windows run, driver DPC behaviour and your card's thermals are not. The crash diagnosis is from forum reports, not your logs.
- **Could fail if:** a GPU job starts outside llama-swap (Ollama or ComfyUI launched by hand), which the guard can only stop through `--stop`; the guard misses Serato's process name (I couldn't confirm the exact `.exe`: run `dj_guard.py --once` with Serato open); or the crashes are PSU transients that no clock cap stops.
- **First step:** install llama-swap with [`prototypes/llama-swap.example.yaml`](prototypes/llama-swap.example.yaml) as the starting config, run [`prototypes/dj_guard.py`](prototypes/dj_guard.py), move one workload (the VLM) behind it, and log `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=power.draw,clocks.gr,temperature.gpu,memory.used --format=csv -l 1` plus HWiNFO memory-junction temperature during the next long Reel Brain batch.

### 3. Measure Laya, then fine-tune and calibrate it
- **Why:** your router is [NandhaKishorM/laya](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya) (Apache-2.0, v0.4.6, last commit 2026-10-12). Its own README is blunt: **the base checkpoints are near chance zero-shot** on typed decisions (0.362 and 0.352 against a 0.318 random and 0.461 majority-class baseline); fine-tuning on the benchmark's training split took the same decisions to **0.766**. They are also **over-confident as shipped** (mean ECE 0.466 → 0.081 after fitting one temperature per question type), and `laya-multilingual` ships with **no fitted temperatures**, so its confidences mean little until you fit them. On CPU, torch's default threading gave a p50 of 9.4 s on a busy laptop; pinning threads (`torch.set_num_threads(<physical cores>)`, `set_num_interop_threads(1)`) brought it to 0.4–0.8 s. Every Hermes message goes through this.
- **Do:** (1) hand-check 200 logged routes and compute accuracy per route; (2) label 2–3k logged messages with a strong teacher (your best local model overnight, or Claude) plus your own corrections, so the labels are not Laya's own guesses; (3) fine-tune with **`laya-train`** (since 0.4): one command from a `text,label` CSV on one device (the 3080 Ti when you're not DJing, or CPU with `--freeze-encoder`); it holds out a calibration slice and fits temperatures and per-option abstention thresholds (`--target-error`, default 10 %) itself, and your messages never leave the PC. Use `--loss soft-ce` (0.790 vs 0.772 for the default loss in Laya's own loss comparison, a separate run from the 0.766 above) and `--shuffle-options` if a question has many options; (4) A/B against the cheap baseline: EmbeddingGemma-300M embeddings + logistic regression on the same labels; (5) score before and after with Laya's own harness, `laya-evals run rows.jsonl --model <checkpoint> --slice language --max-ece 0.1 --json after.json` and `laya-evals compare after.json --baseline before.json`, which records the checkpoint, dataset hash and thresholds so the comparison is honest, and slices Arabic, English and mixed messages separately. Roll out as Laya's `docs/staged-adoption.md` describes: shadow with prediction hooks, compare, set the threshold from held-out data, promote one route at a time. Keep whichever wins.
- **Pitfalls named in the README and open issues:** don't use `true`/`false` or `yes`/`no` as `choice` labels (the model follows the label word); negated requests can flip (e.g. "don't cancel" → `cancel_account` at p=0.9998); more than ~20 options per question get truncated, so split coarse/fine; the fixed 4-epoch default gives a small dataset **too few optimizer updates, and the head collapses to the class prior** ([#963](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/issues/963), open until early stopping merges): read the update budget `laya-train --dry-run` prints (the default run on Laya's 6,000-decision benchmark is 352 updates; 1,100 items give only 72), raise `--epochs` to get near it (the reporter's useful epoch was 17–31), repeat with two or three `--seed`s (6 of 15 identical runs never left the plateau), and use the "positive control" in Laya's `docs/finetune.md` if a run stays at chance. Serving your fine-tune is solved: `Router(models=...)` / `Router.register(...)` take custom checkpoints since 0.3.28 and `laya-serve` reads `LAYA_EXTRA_MODELS` since 0.4.1; only routing *by content* to a checkpoint is still open ([#919](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/issues/919)).
- **Confidence: high** for "measure and fine-tune" (it is what the project's own maintainers say); medium on how much it gains on *your* routes.
- **Could fail if:** labels are too few or too noisy, or your messages mix Arabic and English in ways the multilingual checkpoint reads poorly. Training on your own card avoids uploading anything; if you use Kaggle for a big run, strip anything private first.
- **First step:** export 200 recent routing decisions with the message text and mark each right or wrong. One evening. Score them with [`prototypes/laya_eval.py`](prototypes/laya_eval.py) (accuracy per route, calibration, a confidence gate, and a cheap baseline on the same labels); it also exports the CSV `laya-train` reads.

### 4. Fix Reel Brain's looping JSON at the source: image-token budget, sampling, quant, bounded schema
- **Why:** two open issues match your stack closely. Ollama [#13084](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/13084): Qwen3-VL 4B/8B on **Windows with an RTX 3080** produces "highly unsorted contents" or repeats short sentences once the prompt exceeds `num_ctx`; the reporter's images were 3236×2160 and the logs said "truncating input prompt". [QwenLM/Qwen3-VL#1611](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL/issues/1611): Qwen3-VL loops across runtimes (Transformers, Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp), more with **greedy decoding** and **low (4-bit) quants**; the same author ran GLM-4.6V-Flash on 500 cases with zero loops. A Qwen-VL image costs roughly `h × w / (32 × 32)` tokens, so a full-resolution 1080×1920 frame is ~2,000 tokens and eight of them ~16,000. **And on a card under 24 GB, Ollama's default context is 4K tokens** ([Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/context-length)): unless Reel Brain sets `num_ctx` on every request (its code isn't on GitHub, so I couldn't check), three full-resolution frames already overflow it, which is exactly the truncation in #13084. Check this first.
- **Do:** (1) downscale frames to ≤ 640 px on the long side (a 360×640 frame is ~225 tokens by the formula above), cap at 6–8 per reel, set `num_ctx` explicitly and log prompt tokens per request; (2) use Qwen's recommended Instruct sampling (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, presence penalty 1.5), not temperature 0; (3) Q6_K instead of the 4-bit build (by its 6.1 GB size, Ollama's `qwen3-vl:8b` is 4-bit). Not Q8: Q8_0 (~8.7 GB) + projector (~1.2 GB) + KV cache at 16K (~2.4 GB) is over 12 GB; Q6_K at 8K context is ~9 GB, and 8 downscaled frames need only ~2K tokens; (4) a schema that cannot run away: `maxItems` on every array, `maxLength` on strings, enums, thinking off, a hard cap on output tokens, and **no `$ref`s**: llama.cpp's schema-to-grammar converter (what llama-server and Ollama 0.30+ use) enforces `maxItems`/`maxLength` but documents nested `$ref`s as broken and skips what it can't convert *silently*, and Pydantic emits nested `$ref`s for any nested model or enum (`vlm_json_guard.py` inlines them); validate with Pydantic, retry once, then fall back to "free text → a second small call that only formats JSON"; (5) A/B GLM-4.6V-Flash (9B, MIT per model listings, GGUF available) on the same 20 reels.
- **Ollama itself:** the bug where it ignored repetition penalties ([#15783](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/15783)) was closed in July 2026 as obsoleted by 0.30's move onto llama.cpp, but the umbrella issue [#14493](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14493) is still open and nobody has confirmed penalties work on 0.30+. Check `ollama --version` (update if older than 0.30), diagnose on Ollama (Day 2), rerun the same reels on llama-server behind llama-swap (Day 3), and keep whichever loops less.
- **Confidence: medium.** The issues match your hardware and model; nobody has run your prompts.
- **Could fail if:** the prompts themselves invite open-ended lists or the model needs thinking to read the frames. The bounded schema and the GLM A/B cover those.
- **First step:** record your Ollama version and the prompt-token count for the 20 reels that failed most recently, then rerun them with downscaled frames and the recommended sampling. [`prototypes/vlm_json_guard.py`](prototypes/vlm_json_guard.py) does all of that against Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible server.

### 5. Wire the planner to your FilmCraft fork, headless, and build it with craft-fonts
- **Why:** this is the seam the whole studio hangs on, and nothing had shown the fork could take a plan and return a finished 9:16 clip. [`prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py`](prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py) now does it: plan JSON → `filmcraft-cli mcp` (no window, so the under-tested Windows UI, gap G9, is out of the loop) → a 1080×1920 sequence, cuts from two cameras, Motion scale + anchor keyframes for the reframe, the Serato recording on A1, burned-in captions, H.264 with a loudness target.
- **Do:** (1) sync the fork to upstream's v0.7.2 tag (your `main` was 433 commits behind on 2026-10-13); (2) build `filmcraft-cli` in release mode with `CRAFT_FONTS_DIR` pointing at a clone of [storytold/craft-fonts](https://github.com/storytold/craft-fonts), which FilmCraft's own CLAUDE.md asks for and which makes Arabic captions render with the default font; (3) make the planner emit the plan JSON documented at the top of the script, never FilmCraft command IDs; (4) keep `file.exportOtio` as the escape hatch to Resolve.
- **Arabic captions work in FilmCraft if you name the font (tested).** I burned an Egyptian code-switched caption ("…استعدوا للـ drop") into a headless export from your fork. With the default caption font (Inter) every Arabic letter came out as a "NO GLYPH" box, even with an Arabic font installed, because the fallback ignores installed fonts (upstream [#1475](https://github.com/storytold/filmcraft/issues/1475) reports it for graphics layers; fix PR [#1478](https://github.com/storytold/filmcraft/pull/1478) open, revised 13 Oct; the merged fix for [#395](https://github.com/storytold/filmcraft/issues/395) covered only the editor UI). Two ways rendered correctly (joined letters, right-to-left, the English word at the left end): naming an Arabic face on the track (`captions.setStyle {"track": "C1", "font": "Noto Sans Arabic"}`; on Windows, Noto Sans Arabic (OFL) or Segoe UI), or the craft-fonts build in (2), whose Noto Sans Arabic the default font falls back to. Reproduce with [`prototypes/filmcraft_arabic_captions.jsonl`](prototypes/filmcraft_arabic_captions.jsonl) (screenshots aren't committed: the fork's asset gate needs a licence sidecar per image). Pick the house Arabic face per client in `look.md`; animated Arabic type belongs in the HTML layer.
- **Confidence: high** for the mechanics: the script's test drives real release builds of both your fork's base and upstream v0.7.2 with craft-fonts (identical results: cuts on the planned frames, subject in frame on every reframed frame, captions burned in, audio present; Arabic captions render with the default font in the v0.7.2 + craft-fonts build). **Medium** for your machine: the Windows build and your camera files are untested.
- **Could fail if:** your cameras record 10-bit 4:2:2 (FilmCraft can't decode it on Windows without hardware support, gap G3: transcode to 8-bit 4:2:0 first), or upstream renames commands (pin the tag; the script is ~240 lines to adapt).
- **First step:** build v0.7.2 in a worktree with craft-fonts and run `test_plan_to_filmcraft.py` against it (exact commands in Day 5 of the first week).

### 6. Measure 40 reference clips
- **Why:** I found **no published description** of how Boiler Room, BCCO or HÖR cut their shorts. Guessing produces the "stock" look. Your own whatstheID notes show the method that works for you: measure, then build.
- **Do:** collect ~40 clips (analysis only, never re-published). Run shot detection (PySceneDetect, BSD-3, or FilmCraft's `clip.sceneEditDetection`) and beat tracking (Beat This) on each. Output: cuts per clip, shot length in beats, % of cuts on a downbeat/bar/phrase line, where the drop sits, how long the first shot holds, when text appears and what it says.
- **Hypothesis to test:** many of these clips are one or two fixed-camera shots where the edit is mostly *selection* (start a few bars before the drop, end on the reaction) plus a track-ID caption, not rapid cutting. If true, alternating crowd/DJ cuts is the wrong default.
- **Confidence: high** that it pays off; the numbers it finds are unknown by definition.
- **Could fail if:** the 40 clips mix styles so much that the medians describe nobody (read the spread, and split by channel); strobes still fool shot detection on some clips (check the flagged ones by eye); or some "references" are re-edits by aggregator accounts rather than the original channel's cut.
- **First step:** run [`prototypes/analyze_reference_clips.py`](prototypes/analyze_reference_clips.py) `--strobe-safe` over a folder of ~40 clips; look at the medians. Use `--strobe-safe` on club footage: in the synthetic test, strobes inside one shot turned 7 real cuts into 9 for a plain detector.

### 7. Move Hermes to llama.cpp with expert offload, and cut the tool list by hand
- **Why:** Hermes' own local-runtime design is the right shape for 12 GB: MoE expert weights in system RAM, attention and KV cache on the GPU, ≥64K context ([local-models docs](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/blob/main/website/docs/user-guide/local-models.md)). Run it as your own `llama-server` behind llama-swap, not Hermes' managed runtime, so one process owns the GPU (Top 10 #2). On a 12 GB RTX 3060, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B generates ~35–45 tok/s with expert offload: InsiderLLM (32 GB DDR4, Q4_K_M) measured `--n-cpu-moe 24` fastest at 38.9 tok/s (9.8 GB VRAM), flat to 8K context, on a **headless** card; they note a card that also drives monitors loses ~0.5–1 GB to the desktop, so start one or two layers higher or let `--fit` with a 1.5 GB target choose. [One user](https://x.com/DogukanUrker/status/2077472690156511643) reports ~45 tok/s at `--n-cpu-moe 26`, 150K context and **only 16 GB of RAM**, with `-np 1` and a q8_0 KV cache (read through a search snippet; X won't load here). Your 3080 Ti's extra bandwidth helps less than it seems: the offloaded experts run at your RAM's speed. Hermes' guide says prefilling the fixed prompt and tool schemas "dominates the first turn" on low-VRAM setups, and behind llama-swap every swap brings that first turn back, so shrink the fixed prompt first: `hermes prompt-size` shows it, `hermes tools` and per-profile toolsets cut it.
- **Don't move Qwen 3.5/3.6 onto Ollama instead:** its stock renderer still misses tool calls in reports through September 2026 (2 of 5 relay calls on v0.30.7, 4 of 5 with a `qwen3-coder` renderer override; [#14493](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14493), open). Your current Qwen3-Coder-30B on Ollama uses a different renderer, so it stays as the fallback.
- **Keep Tool Search off with Qwen.** Its bridge tool is named `tool_call`, which collides with the `<tool_call>` tag in Qwen's chat template; Qwen calls the bridge malformed until the guardrail halts the turn ([#110442](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/110442), open; the rename to `invoke_tool` is in an unmerged PR): `hermes config set tools.tool_search.enabled off`.
- **Known Qwen3.6 + llama.cpp issues in Hermes, with workarounds:** pass `--chat-template-file` with the right Qwen template, not only `--jinja`, or output can be garbage ([#29677](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/29677)); leave `capabilities.answer_in_reasoning` unset, because a tool call stranded inside `<think>` still happened in **1 of 23 tool-call turns** ([#129440](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/129440)); `search_files` can loop when the model passes a glob as a regex ([#66129](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/66129)). Expect a few percent of turns to need a retry: "reliable" at 12 GB means "recovers well", not "never fails".
- **Models for the replay:** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (Apache-2.0) with ≥32 GB of RAM (16 GB is reported to work, but not with Serato, Chrome and ComfyUI open); it scores 81.2 on TAU2 tool use against 68.2 for Gemma 4 26B-A4B (vendor-adjacent numbers). Qwen3.5-9B (Apache-2.0, dense, fully on the GPU) with 16 GB. The challenger: **Gemma 4 12B** (Apache-2.0, native function calling, 256K context, a dense size added in June 2026 per secondary write-ups; [ggml-org GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/gemma-4-12B-GGUF)). It should fit entirely on the card ([a VRAM calculator](https://aliteq.com/run-gemma-4-12b-locally) says ~7.8 GB at Q4_K_M and 16K; no measured speed found), and the family has the cleaner Hermes record: #29677 is titled "Qwen/DeepSeek … produce corrupted output (but Gemma works with same config)", and Hermes' guide picks `gemma4:31b` (too big for you) as its reliable tool-caller. No published tool-calling scores for the 12B, so the replay decides.
- **Also:** be on ≥ v0.21.2 (v0.22.0 is current): v0.21.0's session-store rewrite made `state.db` fragile and v0.21.2 closed 44 issues about it. Install with `install.ps1`, not pip (PyPI stops at 0.19.0). Run `hermes backup` nightly (the docs say never to copy `state.db` alone).
- **Confidence: medium.** Docs and issues read directly; speed numbers are from a slower card; the failure rates are single reports.

### 8. Beat grids for your own sets from your own track files
- **Why:** blind beat tracking on a 150 BPM mix fights overlapping kicks in transitions, pitch riding and breakdowns. You have better information. **Serato DJ 4 logs every load** in `master.sqlite` → `history_entry` (artist, title, BPM, key, deck, `start_time`, `played`; verified in the [whats-now-playing reader](https://github.com/whatsnowplaying/whats-now-playing/blob/main/nowplaying/serato/reader.py)). Your library files carry Serato's beat grid (`Serato BeatGrid` GEOB). Align each played track to the set recording (your cross-correlation code plus whatstheID's speed ladder), then transform that track's grid into set time. This is the published "DJ-mix reverse engineering" approach (Schwarz & Fourer; Kim et al., ISMIR 2020).
- **Fallback:** Beat This ([CPJKU/beat_this](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this), MIT, small checkpoints, pip install, no madmom needed) for tracks you don't own. Evidence it suits your genre: a user benchmark on Giant Steps (664 Beatport electronic tracks) in [issue #13](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/13) got **89.3 % strict / 95.0 % octave-tolerant** tempo accuracy, 90.9 / 96.4 % with an ensemble of the *small* models (which did better than the big ones on near-constant tempo). The maintainers confirmed in [issue #16](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/16) that the MIT licence covers the weights. Add an octave-correction prior (120–160 BPM for techno).
- **Recording note:** the MC4000 unlocks **Serato DJ Lite**, which has a History panel but no built-in mix recorder (Pro has one). If your recordings come from OBS or the controller, alignment is the same; only the clock you seed it with differs. On Serato 3.x the history is the older binary `_Serato_/History/Sessions/*.session` format (also parsed by whats-now-playing) instead of `master.sqlite`.
- **Precedent on real mixes:** Kim et al. (ISMIR 2020, [djmix-analysis](https://github.com/mir-aidj/djmix-analysis)) aligned tracks to 1,557 real 1001Tracklists mixes with subsequence DTW, worked example a Boiler Room set; they report DJs rarely change tempo or key much, which keeps stretch ratios small. Their repo has no licence file: read it, don't copy it.
- **Free with it: track-ID captions.** The same history names every track and when it came in, so [`prototypes/track_id_captions.py`](prototypes/track_id_captions.py) writes an "ID: Artist – Title" SRT for any clip window (the first question under every club clip), and the end-to-end test burns it in through FilmCraft.
- **Confidence: medium.** The history fields are verified from code; alignment through heavy EQ/filter/FX is not tested on your sets.
- **Could fail if:** the DJ rides the pitch so the stretch ratio drifts inside a track (fit it piecewise) or the track was played from a streaming service (no file).
- **First step:** list the last session with [`prototypes/serato_set_grid.py`](prototypes/serato_set_grid.py) `history`, then `align` three tracks you own against the recording. In a synthetic mix (track 3 % fast, bass cut for a 30 s blend, crowd noise) it recovered the ratio exactly and the offset to 15 ms.

### 9. Reframe from body pose + an L1-optimal camera path
- **Why:** YuNet finds faces; a DJ looks down at the controller under strobes, so faces drop out exactly when it matters. A real operator frames the body and hands, holds still, then pans deliberately.
- **Do:** detect people and keypoints with RTMPose via [rtmlib](https://github.com/Tau-J/rtmlib) (Apache-2.0, ONNX, light) and keep YuNet as one signal. Choose the subject per shot, then solve the crop path offline as an L1-optimal path (Grundmann et al., "Auto-Directed Video Stabilization with Robust L1 Optimal Camera Paths", the method behind YouTube's stabiliser): the L1 objective produces static holds, constant pans and smooth ease-ins, like a camera operator. Google's AutoFlip (polynomial L2 fit) is the obvious off-the-shelf alternative, but MediaPipe lists it as a legacy solution whose support ended on 2023-03-01. I found MATLAB re-implementations of the L1 paper but no maintained Python one; the linear program is small enough to write with `scipy.optimize.linprog` (HiGHS, BSD) or cvxpy.
- **Avoid:** Ultralytics YOLO (AGPL-3.0) anywhere near the FilmCraft fork.
- **Into FilmCraft, tested:** Motion's `anchor` is in source pixels, so the solved path goes in as anchor keyframes at the path's corners (Douglas–Peucker, ≤ 3 px error). [`prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py`](prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py) does it against a real build of your fork: on a subject sweeping across the frame with noisy, gappy detections, the subject stayed fully in the 9:16 frame on 100 % of reframed frames (a centre crop: 34 %) with 8–14 keyframes per 3-second shot. **Pitfall the test caught:** the L1 path keeps the subject's *centre* inside its safe zone, so the margin must be at least half the subject's width (a DJ's body, not a face) or the edges leave the frame; at the old 60 px margin only 91 % of frames kept the whole subject.
- **Confidence: medium.** The path and the FilmCraft side are tested on synthetic footage; detection quality on dark, strobing club footage is not.
- **Could fail if:** body detection drops out in strobe and darkness as often as faces do (then a fixed, slightly wide crop on the booth beats any tracking, which #6's "selection, not cutting" hypothesis would also favour), or the subject isn't one person (B2B sets: frame the pair, not the louder one).
- **First step:** run rtmlib on one 60-second DJ clip and plot face-detection vs body-detection coverage per frame; feed the body centres to [`prototypes/l1_reframe_path.py`](prototypes/l1_reframe_path.py). On a synthetic swaying DJ with 15 px jitter and 10 % missed detections, its path held still 84 % of the time with no direction changes, where a moving-average follow changed direction 189 times.

### 10. Write cue points into Serato, and grids only where Serato's are wrong
- **Why:** Serato analyses a grid on import, and your tempo already matches Serato's to 0.01 BPM on 17 tracks, so rewriting grids buys little. The value is (a) **cue points Serato never sets**: `analyze.py` already finds intro end, drop and outro start, prep you do by hand today; and (b) **locked grids where the two disagree** and your fit is clean (your notes have one: "Twice or it's Luck", Serato 144 vs your 140). [rb2serato](https://github.com/SM4RTENHEIMER/rb2serato) (MIT, Sep 2026) has encoders for `Serato Markers2`, `Serato Markers_` and `Serato BeatGrid` that it says match Serato DJ Pro 2.x–4.x byte for byte (stdlib-only `seratotags.py`), and documents Serato 4's import quirks (crate header exactly `1.0/Serato ScratchLive Crate`, WAV typed `wave`, Serato closed while writing).
- **What it doesn't do (read in its code):** FLAC, your default format (rb2serato writes ID3 and M4A, serato-tools only MP3/AIFF; Serato keeps FLAC data in Vorbis comments `SERATO_BEATGRID` / `SERATO_MARKERS_V2` per [Holzhaus/serato-tags](https://github.com/Holzhaus/serato-tags)); a grid anchor (whatstheID stores the BPM and no beat position, `_comb_tempo` finds the phase and throws it away, and 0.01 BPM off is ~27 ms of drift by the end of a six-minute track); Windows (its writer shells out to macOS `cp -c`). And cues share one tag with your hand-set cues, loops, track colour and BPM lock, so a naive write wipes your prep.
- **Prototype ([`serato_writeback.py`](prototypes/serato_writeback.py), tested):** merges cues into free slots (skips one within 250 ms of yours; keeps loops, colour and lock; refuses Serato Flip data it can't re-encode); fits anchor and BPM to every kick transient across the track and refuses a drifting tempo; writes MP3/WAV/AIFF and FLAC by copy → tag → audio-hash check → replace. On a recorded 125 BPM loop the anchor landed 0.9 ms from the kick (124.9997 BPM; librosa's `beat_track` said 123.05, your own lag-grid finding); 180 s of synthetic techno at 132.004 BPM analysed as 132.0 came back 132.0042. Tags read back identically through its decoder and through serato-tools (MP3, FLAC), audio bytes unchanged. It imports mutagen (GPL), so it belongs in whatstheID, not the fork.
- **MP3 timing:** Serato's MP3 timeline differs from FFmpeg/libmad decoding by ~24–26 ms (Mixxx corrects Serato cues by −26 ms at 44.1 kHz and −24 ms at 48 kHz; GPL, read for the numbers only); FLAC and WAV don't. Measure yours with `compare` on MP3s Serato analysed and pass `--offset-ms`.
- **Confidence: medium.** Two independent parsers agree on the bytes and the anchor is exact on one recorded loop, but nothing has been loaded by Serato; rb2serato has 1 star; the FLAC layout predates Serato 4. Serato 4 keeps its library in SQLite and re-imports `database V2` and crates at start-up when they change; nobody documents whether it re-reads rewritten tags on a track it already knows, which is exactly your case.
- **Could fail if:** Serato keeps its cached grid for known tracks (then each write needs a re-import), Serato 4 reads FLAC Serato fields differently from the pre-4 notes, or your MP3 offset varies by encoder (measure it per source: your downloads vs your bought files).
- **First step:** back up `_Serato_` and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Serato`; with Serato closed, run `serato_writeback.py write --cues-only` with whatstheID's cues on **copies** of three files Serato has never seen (one MP3, one FLAC, one WAV) and on one FLAC it already knows and you've set cues on; reopen Serato and check the new cues land where written and yours are untouched. Then try `anchor` + a grid write on the one track where Serato's BPM is wrong. If the known track doesn't show the new cues, the write-back needs a re-import step, which changes the 2–3 day estimate; if the fresh FLAC shows no grid, the FLAC layout is wrong and FLAC waits.

**House look per client (not in the Top 10: still low confidence, because taste can't be checked without you)**
- **Why:** "stock AI" is mostly the absence of decisions: default type, eased slides, a new colour per scene. A look written down as rules can be checked by your existing motion-graphics QA step; taste that lives in a prompt cannot.
- **Do:** per client, one `look.md` (also the Inspiration Canvas format, 2.5): one display face and one text face from libre foundries (Velvetyne: OFL or GPL per font; Collletttivo: free for commercial use; Use & Modify: a curated list, check each), never changed mid-series; a type scale; a palette sampled from *their* footage (dominant colours + one accent); and a **motion grammar on the bar grid**: things appear and hold on downbeats, move only across bar lines, hard-cut on the drop, no ease-in-out slides. Use [motionmaxxing](https://github.com/Tejashmakwana/motionmaxxing) (Apache-2.0) as the rubric: its tells ("everything is a slide", typed-out headlines with a caret, a new flat colour per scene) and counter-rules come from 53 frame-accurate professional motion moments, where pros used **3,392 transform keyframes against 584 opacity fades** (things move and cut; they rarely fade), though on brand films, not club footage.
- **The rhythm half is checkable now:** [`prototypes/beat_lint.py`](prototypes/beat_lint.py) lints a HyperFrames composition against the track's grid (clip starts and ends on beats, titles marked `data-beat="bar"` on bar lines, relative starts resolved, GSAP tween positions on beats) and flags in-out eased moves and typed-out text; exit code 1 means a finding. Tested on a synthetic composition only.
- **Confidence: low.** Taste is the least verifiable claim here. To raise it, show the collective two versions of the same clip (current graphics vs a `look.md` version that passes the lint) blind, and count which they'd post; if the house look wins, it belongs in the Top 10.
- **Could fail if:** the rules are followed and the result is merely tidy. The reference measurements (Top 10 #6) and your eye on every first clip of a series are the backstop.
- **First step:** write `look.md` for the collective you want to pitch, using their existing flyers as the source.

**Shot picking from two synced angles (prototype: [`shot_picker.py`](prototypes/shot_picker.py), tested).** Per bar and angle it scores sharpness (Laplacian variance), exposure, motion and strobe flashes (pose, hands-up and taste scores can be added per bar), then picks an angle per bar by dynamic programming: cuts only on bar lines, each shot at least 2 bars and preferably ≤ 4 (a soft limit, so a shot runs long only when the other angle is unusable), crowd angle on the drop bar and the next (the reaction lags the drop). It writes a plan for `plan_to_filmcraft.py`. In its test (two synthetic angles, one dark for 4 bars, the other out of focus for 6) it avoided both bad stretches and put the crowd on the drop; a hard 4-bar maximum had forced a cut to the blurred angle, which is why the maximum is a cost, not a wall. Take the limits from your Top 10 #6 medians, not my defaults. Later, learn your taste: ~200 keep/skip labels and a logistic head on frozen SigLIP embeddings (Apache-2.0); an IEEE paper on multi-camera editing learned an editor's preferences from 33 scenes with CLIP + SVM. The LLM only chooses among pre-scored candidates; it never watches video.

**Planner and judge in 12 GB**
- **Planner: the Hermes agent model, not a third model.** Whichever wins Top 10 #7's replay (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-9B on 16 GB of RAM, or Gemma 4 12B) can read images once its own projector is loaded (the commented `--mmproj` lines in the llama-swap example; Qwen3.5 projectors don't fit Qwen3.6). - **Don't let a model judge taste.** Timeline-Bench (arXiv 2609.35143, Sep 2026) gave agents 56 realistic raw-footage editing tasks: the best resolved 26.8 % (15 of 56; 95 % interval 15.8–40.3 %), the average 14 %, and 562 of 771 failed runs failed *only* the quality test (calibrated on 2,582 blind judgments by 43 editors), which the authors attribute to agents judging footage through stills and transcripts rather than craft. Pairwise LLM judges still flip with presentation order in up to 20 % of cases (J1, 2025). Use the VLM as a **defect checker** with yes/no questions on contact sheets (text over a face? cut mid-word? black or frozen frames? drop missing? first frame dark?) and keep the taste pick for yourself or for Trial Reels.
- **Keep the planner's output tool-neutral.** The plan should be a small JSON of cuts (source, in, duration), subject samples, captions and export settings, not FilmCraft command IDs, which change fast (your fork fell 433 commits behind upstream). [`plan_to_filmcraft.py`](prototypes/plan_to_filmcraft.py) is the FilmCraft back end for exactly that shape. For a fallback editor, FilmCraft's `file.exportOtio` writes the built timeline as OpenTimelineIO, which Resolve imports, so a FilmCraft build that misbehaves on Windows costs a day, not a rewrite. Pin the fork to a release tag (v0.7.2; Top 10 #5) rather than tracking `main`.

**Laya (router)** — see Top 10 #3 for the evidence.
- Labels have to come from outside Laya: your corrections (a "wrong route" button in Telegram), implicit signals (you re-ask, you switch model by hand, the task fails), and a teacher model labelling logged messages overnight. Fine-tuning on Laya's own logged guesses teaches it nothing.
- Route by calibrated `answer_confidence`; below the threshold, fall back to the main LLM and queue the message for labelling.
- Keep the EmbeddingGemma-300M + logistic-regression baseline running in shadow mode: it costs milliseconds on CPU and tells you whether Laya's fine-tune is earning its 0.4–0.8 s.
- Question design: one coarse question (which agent/tool family) then a fine one, ≤ ~20 options each, opaque labels (`A`/`B`) rather than yes/no words.
- **Give the route a job that saves GPU time.** Hermes plugins can't switch the model per turn: `pre_llm_call` can only inject context and `pre_tool_call` can only block or approve (Hermes plugin docs). So a route has to act *in front of* Hermes. The payoff that matters on 12 GB: send each message to a Hermes profile with only the toolset that route needs (vault, media, coding, chat; `hermes profile`, per-profile toolsets). Prefill of the fixed prompt dominates the first turn after every swap (Top 10 #7), so a vault message answered by a profile with five vault tools instead of every MCP server is faster on the same model. Measure it with `hermes prompt-size` per profile.
- **Hermes' side-jobs also hit the GPU.** Compression, vision, approval scoring (the `smart` approvals mode), MCP tool routing, session titles, skill search and the curator are "auxiliary" model slots that default to the main model. Leave them on `auto` (the already-loaded model, so nothing swaps); point `auxiliary.vision` at the agent model only if it has its projector loaded, otherwise every image swaps the VLM in.

**Classification and repo linking**
- **Why:** kNN at 63 % is a weak baseline. A logistic-regression "linear probe" on the same embeddings often beats kNN once there are enough labels per folder (measure it below); showing the top 3 turns a 63 % top-1 into a much higher hit rate for one click. For repos, a published study (SONAD) found LLMs over-accept candidates (precision 68 %, recall 97 %) while a feature-based verifier reached 82 % precision.
- **Measure before changing anything:** [`prototypes/collections_probe.py`](prototypes/collections_probe.py) takes the embeddings and folder labels you already have and reports, under the same 5-fold cross-validation, kNN (k = 1/5/15), a logistic-regression probe, the probe's top-3, an "ask me when unsure" rule (how many it sends you, how accurate the rest are), per-folder accuracy and the most-confused folder pairs. On synthetic embeddings with two overlapping folders it showed the expected shape (kNN 0.75–0.81, probe 0.86, top-3 0.999; the rule sent 13 % to you and was 91 % right on the rest), but that is synthetic; your 63 % is the baseline to beat.
- **Do:** (a) re-label 50 random reels blind to find your own consistency ceiling; (b) linear probe + top-3 + LLM rerank only when the top-2 margin is small; (c) for repos, three tiers ending in "no repo" (many tools in reels are closed SaaS products): the prototype below. - **Cue points and phrases:** [CUE-DETR](https://github.com/ETH-DISCO/cue-detr) (MIT code, 2024) detects DJ cue points as objects on a spectrogram and ships the EDM-CUE dataset (4,710 tracks, ~21k cues placed by professional DJs). Its authors found most training tracks use **16-bar phrases, then 8-bar**, so don't hard-code 32. Check the checkpoint/dataset licence before shipping anything built on them.
- **Don't rebuild conversion:** Lexicon (paid DJ library manager) has had a **free plan for importing and converting libraries between DJ apps** since v1.9 (Sep 2025) and supports Serato 4's database since v1.10. whatstheID's grid analysis tuned for techno is the part worth building; app-to-app conversion is solved.
- **Grid quality:** fit to the kick transient and check the offset per format (Top 10 #10). rb2serato measured Serato's grid a median 8 ms ahead of Rekordbox's on 1,563 MP3s, so apps disagree by a few ms even when both are right.
- **Downbeats/phrases (prototype: [`downbeat_phase.py`](prototypes/downbeat_phase.py), tested):** on any beat grid, score the four bar phases on claps/snares on 2 and 4 and on bar-level novelty (sections change on bar lines). Claps alone can only tell {1, 3} from {2, 4}, never beat 1 from beat 3, so a loop with no structural change comes back `half_bar_ambiguous` instead of a guess. Phrase starts count in 8-bar steps from the track's first bar, with novelty as a second opinion flagged when it disagrees: on a recorded loop, "the bar after the biggest change" was a bar late, because a crash on a phrase's first bar and a fill on its last both make one bar stand out. Synthetic 150 BPM techno: right phase in 6 of 6 seeds with structure, claps-only always flagged; the recorded 125 BPM loop: phase 0, phrases at 0 and 15.36 s. Full tracks untested. Half-time D&B: prefer 170–176 over 85–88 by genre prior.
- **IDs in long mixes:** segment first (novelty), then query the middle of each segment where one track plays alone, instead of a fixed 60 s grid; keep the speed ladder.
- **Replace shazamio with AudD's enterprise endpoint as the cloud fallback** (5b has the evidence and the cost).
- **Visualiser:** drive shaders from beat/bar/phrase *phase* from your analysis, not raw FFT amplitude: visuals lock to the music's structure. Butterchurn (MIT, Milkdrop in WebGL) is a reference; Hydra is AGPL.

**Avoid:** Panako and Olaf (both AGPL-3.0) as code; read their papers for the tempo-invariant ideas. PeakNetFP ([code](https://github.com/guillemcortes/peaknetfp), ISMIR-era 2025, BMAT/Deezer/UPF) reports >90 % top-1 for time-stretch factors from 50 % to 200 %, but its repository is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**: personal use only, never in something you sell or share commercially. Your query-side speed ladder already covers ±8 %, the range of a Technics-style pitch fader, which is about as far as techno DJs push a record.

**Licences in your current whatstheID stack (checked on PyPI):** fastapi, uvicorn, pydantic (MIT/BSD), yt-dlp (Unlicense), shazamio (MIT), librosa (ISC) are permissive; **mutagen, which `app/tags.py` uses, is GPL-2.0-or-later**, and serato-tools requires it too. For a personal tool that changes nothing. If you share whatstheID with other DJs as a download, license it GPL-compatible (simplest: GPL-3.0) or replace mutagen; nothing from it can go into the MIT/Apache FilmCraft fork.

**Soulseek-first downloads (not on `main` yet):** slskd is AGPL-3.0; calling its HTTP API from whatstheID as a separate process does not make whatstheID AGPL, but bundling or modifying slskd would. For a tool you share with other DJs, automated P2P downloading is also the part most likely to draw complaints: keep it a user-configured option, not a default.

**Sharing it with other DJs: the field is not empty.** TrackSniff (web, free 50 IDs/month, $9–19/month; mixed accuracy in one academy's test), Mixprism, audioscout.io and AudD (says it handles hours-long mixes; $2–5 per 1,000 requests after 300 free) already do "paste a mix, get a tracklist". What none of them do is what whatstheID already does: **fingerprint against the DJ's own library** (your `fingerprint.py` notes that Shazam named 0 of 6 underground records and AudD 3) and write cues back into Serato. Lead with that; treat cloud ID as a fallback.

### 2.5 Inspiration Canvas (idea stage)

**Recommendation: do not build a canvas app yet. Build the board format first, inside your Obsidian vault.**
- Store boards as **JSON Canvas** (`.canvas`, open spec v1.0, MIT, what Obsidian Canvas uses): `file` nodes for images and videos, `text`, `link` and `group` nodes, edges for relations ([spec](https://github.com/obsidianmd/jsoncanvas/blob/main/spec/1.0.md)). Any LLM can read and write it; Obsidian renders it for free; Kinopio imports and exports it; there are Python (PyJSONCanvas), TypeScript and Rust (`jsoncanvas` crate) libraries if you ever build your own viewer.
- A "look pack" = a folder with the images, a `look.md` (palette, type, motion rules, what to avoid) and a `.canvas` board. Creative Studio reads `look.md`.
- Imports: Instagram via the official data export (same as Reel Brain); Pinterest via its data export or API v5 Trial access (reading your own boards and pins is allowed on Trial; approval is reported as slow).
- If you later want a real app: tldraw needs a licence key in production but runs free in development mode on `localhost`; Excalidraw is MIT. A FilmCraft extension would mean egui work in a Rust app whose Windows runtime is already the weak spot: no.
- **The paid option that may simply win:** Eagle (eagle.cool), a local image/video reference manager with a browser extension and plugin system, **$34.95 one-time for two devices**, 30-day trial. If what you want is "collect and tag fast", it already does that; a small exporter to `.canvas` + `look.md` (below) gives Claude the read access. Before writing that exporter, try **EaglePack Importer**, an Obsidian community plugin (by Kerekes Stefan) that turns an Eagle `.eaglepack` export into one note per item with the image embedded plus a gallery note per pack, mirroring Eagle's folders. It is a lead, not a recommendation: I found its listing but not its source repository, so its licence and upkeep are unknown. If the plugin disappoints, write the exporter against Eagle's **official local Web API** ([docs](https://developer.eagle.cool/web-api); v2 at `http://localhost:41595/api/v2/`, requests from localhost are trusted with no token, and `/api/v2/playground` shows the live response shape) rather than its library folder, whose on-disk layout (`images/<id>.info/metadata.json` per third-party tools) is undocumented and can change. Either way the exporter is a few dozen lines: items with tags, source URL and annotation → `.canvas` nodes and a `look.md`.

1. **llama-swap in front of every GPU server, plus the DJ guard** (Top 10 #2): one place to load, unload and measure, instead of Ollama, Hermes' runtime and ComfyUI each holding their own copy of the card.
2. **One small labelled set per project, kept in the vault:** 200 Laya routes, 20 failing reels + 50 blind re-labels, 40 reference clips + your keep/skip shot labels, your 17 Serato reference tracks (BPM and key, already in whatstheID). Every change gets measured against them, which is how whatstheID got its tempo error to 0.01 BPM.
3. **One vault, one set of conventions:** the Obsidian vault is the knowledge store for everything: Claude Code, Reel Brain and the canvas reach it through one MCP server (MCPVault), Hermes through its bundled `obsidian` and `llm-wiki` skills over the same files. You already write to the vault via `learn-to-vault` / claude-obsidian (Karpathy LLM-wiki pattern); don't add a second memory database that drifts from it.
4. **One verification module** for "does this search hit really match": whatstheID's `verify.py` rules reused for Reel Brain's tool→repo links.
5. **Shared audio analysis:** whatstheID's comb-filter tempo and key code, Beat This, and Serato readers serve both whatstheID and Creative Studio.
6. **Shared embeddings:** EmbeddingGemma for the Laya baseline, Reel Brain's collections and canvas search. Licence: the **Gemma Terms of Use**, not Apache (unlike Gemma 4): commercial use is allowed, but Google's prohibited-use policy applies and can change. Fine for your own tools; if you ever ship the embeddings inside something you sell, read the terms or swap in an Apache-2.0 embedder.
7. **One vision-language model where possible:** the Hermes agent model doubles as Creative Studio's planner, and if it also wins Reel Brain's A/B (2.3) it does that job too. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Gemma 4 12B are the two candidates for all three jobs. Fewer models means fewer swaps on one card.
8. **One security rule across the vault.** Reel Brain writes text that strangers wrote (captions, transcripts, on-screen text) into the vault, and Hermes reads the vault with a shell, MCP tools and a Telegram front door. That is the classic indirect prompt-injection path. Hermes' defaults already defend most of it (checked in its code): the gateway denies everyone until an allowlist is set, `approvals.mode` is `smart`, and cron, single-query and unattended runs deny dangerous commands. So the rule is mostly *don't loosen them*: `TELEGRAM_ALLOWED_USERS` = your ID only (the QR setup writes it), never `/yolo` or `GATEWAY_ALLOW_ALL_USERS` on the gateway; give agents that only need to read the vault a second [MCPVault](https://github.com/bitbonsai/mcpvault) instance started with `--read-only`; keep reel notes in their own folder and write "untrusted content from a saved reel" at the top of each one. A repo found in a reel is a lead, not an install: installing it is always your decision, never a plan step an agent acts on alone.

## 5. What I got wrong along the way

The biggest calls the audits killed or reversed (details and dates in `audit-log.md`):

1. **"Ollama ignores repetition penalties, so switch servers to stop the looping JSON."** True once, fixed in Ollama 0.30.0 (July 2026). The likelier causes on your exact stack are image tokens overflowing `num_ctx` (an open issue reproduced on an RTX 3080 under Windows), greedy decoding and 4-bit quants. *Watch for:* bug reports age fast; check the closing comment, not just the title.
2. **"Turn on Hermes Tool Search to shrink the prompt."** It breaks Qwen models: the bridge tool is named `tool_call`, the same word as Qwen's template tag. *Watch for:* features that work with the vendor's default model and not with yours.
3. **"Let a local VLM judge rank the three variants."** Agents judging edits from stills fail mostly on craft (Timeline-Bench), and pairwise judges flip with presentation order. Demoted to a defect checker. *Watch for:* using a model to grade taste.
4. **"Write a GPU broker", then "free ComfyUI through llama-swap when Serato starts".** llama-swap already does the brokering, ComfyUI included. And my DJ guard's ComfyUI call went through llama-swap's `/comfyui/` route, which *starts* ComfyUI: in a test against the real binary, DJ mode ended with ComfyUI on the GPU. The guard now uses a `dj` profile plus re-unloading. *Watch for:* building infrastructure before searching for it, and trusting glue code you haven't run against the real thing.
5. **"Replace Laya with embeddings + logistic regression", then "fine-tune it for fewer epochs on small data".** The first was written before I knew Laya is built to be fine-tuned (the real problem is that it runs zero-shot). The second was backwards: Laya's own issue #963 shows the 4-epoch default gives a small dataset too few updates and the head collapses to the class prior; small data needs *more* epochs. *Watch for:* advice about a component nobody has looked at, and paraphrasing an issue instead of reading its numbers.
6. **"rb2serato already did the risky half of the Serato write-back."** Its encoders are still the best code I found (one star, one author), but it writes no FLAC (your default format), it can't run on Windows as written, whatstheID has no grid anchor to give it, and the item was about grids, which Serato already makes; the value is cues, and a naive cue write would have wiped the cues you set by hand. My own replacement then went wrong the same way: its kick detector was exact on synthetic kicks and 23 ms late on a recorded one. *Watch for:* a README standing in for reading the code against your own files, and synthetic tests standing in for real audio.
7. **"The collective is the first income."** Local reel editing goes for as little as 100 EGP; it is a portfolio play. The dollars are in Arabic-dialect clips for remote clients.
8. **"Qwen3.6 runs at ~36 tok/s on a 12 GB card", then "up to 47 tok/s at `--n-cpu-moe 18`".** The first was untraceable; the second used a setting that can't hold Q4_K_M experts in 12 GB, and my example config's `--n-cpu-moe 22` would probably have run out of VRAM too (the traceable 38.9 tok/s was also measured on a headless card, which yours isn't). Same family of error later: a "~90 tok/s" for Gemma 4 12B that no source supports, and a $0.40/hour Scribe price that had fallen to $0.22. *Watch for:* numbers without the quant, context, hardware and date they were measured at.
9. **"FilmCraft can't do Arabic; set Arabic in the HTML layer."** Based on an upstream bug title. I built the fork and burned an Egyptian caption into an export: with the caption font set to an Arabic face it renders correctly; only the default font fails. (Smaller catch in the same area: FilmCraft drops VP9 alpha, so overlays go in as ProRes 4444, not WebM.) *Watch for:* bug titles standing in for a test.
10. **"QwenCleo-ASR covers the Egyptian-Arabic caption job."** It covers the words, not their timing: Qwen's forced aligner skips Arabic, and the obvious substitute's default model is non-commercial. Word-by-word captions need a second, commercially licensed aligner. *Watch for:* a benchmark on the easy half of a job (WER) standing in for the whole job (timed captions).
